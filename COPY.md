## 6. From Learning to Classrooms

### Final Selected Copy
- **Eyebrow**: From Learning to Classrooms
- **Headline**: How the work actually reaches a child.
- **Subtitle**: A disciplined, student-run operational loop combined with a structured four-session school curriculum.
- **Part A: The Campus Operating Loop**:
  1. *Identify & Connect*: Student outreach leads map government schools in their district, meet with the headmaster, and secure classroom time slots.
  2. *Prepare & Train*: Volunteers rehearse age-appropriate analogies, prepare bilingual prompt sheets in Telugu & English, and test offline demo kits.
  3. *Enter the Classroom*: Student teams arrive with shared tablets and demo setups, guiding students through active, screen-shared experimentation.
  4. *Listen & Document*: Every question, student artwork, and teacher reflection is logged in verified session reports with signed attendance.
  5. *Refine & Loop*: Learnings from one classroom directly improve the next session across all 9 collegiate campus chapters.
- **Part B: The 4-Session School Journey**:
  - **Session 1: AI Awareness** (Demystifying the machine) — What is AI, where does it live, and how is it different from normal software? Separating myth from reality through everyday analogies. *Outcome: Students understand that AI learns from examples and is built by people, not magic.*
  - **Session 2: AI Conversations & Prompt Engineering** (Hands-on prompting in local languages) — Writing descriptive prompts in Telugu and English. Exploring how clarity, detail, and context shape generated images and text. *Outcome: Every student creates at least two original visual generations using their own ideas.*
  - **Session 3: AI Safety, Ethics & Future Careers** (Critical evaluation & digital responsibility) — Spotting AI hallucinations, understanding data privacy, recognizing deepfakes, and discovering careers in tech. *Outcome: Students develop critical thinking to question AI outputs rather than believing everything on screen.*
  - **Session 4: Using AI for Building & Problem Solving** (Solving community challenges) — Students work in small teams to brainstorm how AI can solve real issues in their school, agriculture, or village health. *Outcome: Teams present their AI problem-solving concepts and receive Certificate of Completion.*

## 9. What Changes in a Classroom

### Final Selected Copy
- **Eyebrow**: Observable Outcomes
- **Headline**: What actually changes in a classroom?
- **Subtitle**: We convert every session feature into an observable outcome — putting the student first, before the organization.
- **6 Outcome Cards**:
  1. *Hands-on tablet workshops instead of blackboard lectures* **so students can touch the tools, write prompts directly, and see their own ideas materialize on screen.** (Observed: Students overcome technology hesitation within the first 15 minutes.)
  2. *Bilingual prompt instruction in Telugu and English* **so students can think, experiment, and create in the mother tongue they feel most natural and confident in.** (Observed: Over 70% of first prompts are formulated in Telugu before trying English.)
  3. *Practical lessons on AI hallucinations and biases* **so students can question computer outputs rather than treating digital screens as infallible sources of truth.** (Observed: Students actively challenge incorrect AI answers in classroom fact-check exercises.)
  4. *College-age mentors from nearby campuses* **so students can see relatable role models and ask honest questions about college, coding, and technology careers.** (Observed: Students stay behind after the bell to ask about high-school subject streams.)
  5. *Small-group community problem-solving activities* **so students can discover that technology is a creative problem-solving tool for their own village or neighbourhood.** (Observed: Teams propose ideas ranging from crop pest scanners to bilingual exam helpers.)
  6. *Permanent printed prompt guides and student workbooks* **so students can revisit prompt structures, share concepts with siblings, and continue learning after volunteers leave.** (Observed: Teachers report students referring to workbooks during subsequent computer lab hours.)

## 13. FAQ

### Final Selected Copy
- **Eyebrow**: Frequently Asked Questions
- **Headline**: Clear answers to essential questions.
- **Subtitle**: Everything you need to know about the student-led model, NIAT's role, and how we operate in schools.
- **5 Mandatory Q&As**:
  1. **Who is behind Teach AI for India?**  
     *Answer*: Teach AI for India is founded, organized, and delivered by undergraduate students at NIAT. College students form campus chapters, design bilingual session plans, coordinate with local government schools, and travel to classrooms as volunteer facilitators. Central guidance ensures child safety protocols, rigorous curriculum standards, and verified documentation across all chapters.
  2. **What is NIAT's role?**  
     *Answer*: NIAT is the learning environment and academic ecosystem where our student volunteers master advanced software, AI fundamentals, and engineering principles. Teach AI for India is what the students chose to build using that knowledge. NIAT supports the student initiative with training environments and ecosystem encouragement, but TAI is student-led and student-run.
  3. **Is this a one-time activity or a structured program?**  
     *Answer*: It is a structured program. While we offer introductory demo sessions to break the ice, partner schools participate in a structured four-session journey: 1. AI Awareness, 2. AI Conversations & Prompt Engineering, 3. AI Safety, Ethics & Future Careers, and 4. Using AI for Building & Problem Solving. Each school cohort is tracked across this progression.
  4. **Does it actually reach students in need?**  
     *Answer*: Yes. We focus specifically on government and community schools across Telangana and Andhra Pradesh where students have zero prior access to generative AI or modern digital creation tools. Every session is documented with photographs, headmaster acknowledgements, and verified student attendance counts.
  5. **How can our school, company, or institution partner with you?**  
     *Answer*: You can partner with us in multiple ways: schools can invite our campus teams for free four-session workshop cohorts; educators can provide curriculum feedback; tech companies can donate tablets or provide guest mentors; and organizations can help underwrite student workbooks or rural transport. Reach out via the conversation form below to get started.
